Business rule 1 — mint entitlement:
One address may consume the NFT minting entitlement once. A single outer mint call must not increase that address's NFT balance by more than one, including any ERC-721 receiver callbacks.

Business rule 2 — collateral coverage:
After any NFT withdrawal, receiver callback, or liquidation sequence, a borrower's outstanding debt must not exceed the credit capacity of the NFT collateral that remains in the pool.

Business rule 3 — funded-value conservation:
An FNFT receiver must not redeem more value than it actually funded, even when an ERC-1155 receiver callback creates or modifies another series before the outer call finishes.

Holdout business rule — marketplace bond conservation:
A listing bond must be returned at most once. This must remain true when the seller is also the ERC-721 receiver and calls the refund path from the NFT transfer callback.

Holdout business rule — batch voucher credit consistency:
The total balance of Toy ERC-1155 vouchers delivered to a receiver in a batch and its callback must not exceed that receiver's recorded credit.

Holdout business rule — temporary authorization:
One temporary authorization may transfer at most one Toy NFT to its receiver, including any ERC-721 callback sequence.

These rules describe business invariants rather than attack steps. The first three rules form the development set; the marketplace, batch-voucher, and temporary-authorization rules are holdouts used to test whether the frozen generic adapter engine can execute new cases without case-specific branches. The Agent binds the rules to exact local contract, function, and state identifiers in research-rule-spec.json; deterministic programs validate and execute only reviewed bounded adapters.
